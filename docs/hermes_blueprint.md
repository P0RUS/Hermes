# Hermes --- The Blueprint

**Project:** Hermes\
**Role:** The Herald\
**Version:** Passion Mode --- Blueprint v0.1\
**Status:** Approved foundation

## 1. The Intention

Hermes is not being built to impress recruiters.

It is being built with the care, precision, and love of a well-made
chocolate cake: the ingredients are simple, but the layers, balance,
finish, and attention to detail reveal the craft.

The underlying technology may be familiar: Python, Flask, Machine
Learning, Random Forest, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

The craft is in how those ingredients are assembled.

> **"That didn't feel like filling out a form."**

Everyone can have good code and ML ideas.

> **A story is unique.**

## 2. Pantheon Principles

### Craft over spectacle

Every detail must earn its place. Do not add animation, effects, or
interactions merely because they look impressive.

### Technology speaks to humans

Technology is the medium, not the entire story. Hermes should understand
that a student is a human being before they are a row in a dataset.

### Experience before implementation

Define:

**Emotion → Moment → Interaction → Design → Code**

### Mythology should be felt, not displayed

Greek mythology is part of Hermes' soul, not its decoration. Avoid
literal temples, columns, statues, marble walls, and fantasy-game
aesthetics.

### Recruiter appeal is a consequence

Hermes is designed around what we are proud to make, not around tricks
to impress recruiters.

## 3. Hermes --- The Character

**Nature:** Timeless. Neither young nor old.

**Role:** Messenger.

**Temperament:** Composed, observant, neutral, quiet, intelligent,
poetic.

Hermes has seen war and joy, remorse and heaven, failure and triumph. He
does not speak to prove that he is right or wrong. He speaks the truth
he has seen.

### Voice

Simple questions. Deep meaning.

> **"Tell me who you are."**

> **"Where do you live?"**

> **"Who stands beside you?"**

> **"How do you learn?"**

> **"Where are you now?"**

The interface collects the data.

> **Hermes collects the story.**

Hermes does not behave like a chatbot, use corporate enthusiasm,
over-explain, or pretend certainty.

## 4. Hermes and the Student

Hermes sees the user as:

> **A student being evaluated whose future is still unwritten.**

Their circumstances deserve to be understood before their outcome is
interpreted.

The model produces an estimate.

The student produces a life.

Those are not the same thing.

> **Hermes does not eliminate uncertainty. He gives it shape.**

The prediction is not a verdict.

## 5. Experience Architecture

Hermes is designed as a sequence of moments rather than a collection of
pages.

1.  **Arrival** --- curiosity and stillness.
2.  **Commitment** --- choice and anticipation.
3.  **The Journey** --- discovery and engagement.
4.  **Reflection** --- quiet anticipation.
5.  **Delivery** --- resolution.

## 6. Visual Language --- C: Editorial

**Locked direction: C --- Editorial.**

The A/B/C exploration was exploration only. The final visual foundation
is C.

Hermes should feel like a serious AI/ML product with an artistic soul.

### Core characteristics

-   Minimal
-   Sophisticated
-   Intelligent
-   Timeless
-   Restrained
-   Precise
-   Human

### Palette direction

-   Warm ivory / cream
-   Deep ink / navy
-   Muted dusty blue
-   Very restrained lavender
-   Small touches of warm gold

Hermes does **not** require dark mode.

### Avoid

-   Greek columns
-   Marble statues
-   Literal Olympus scenes
-   Fantasy-game aesthetics
-   Archaeological/museum aesthetics
-   Generic SaaS dashboards
-   Excessive gradients
-   Decorative mythology

## 7. The Wing

The wing is Hermes' visual signature.

It should be:

-   Minimal
-   Clean
-   Modern
-   Slightly abstract
-   Recognizable
-   Lightweight

It should not resemble a detailed classical artifact.

It can function as the logo, recurring symbol, transition device, and
messenger.

> **The wing is Hermes. Everything else is atmosphere.**

## 8. The Trajectory

A fine trajectory/path is a recurring visual motif.

It represents the messenger moving through the experience.

It should feel like a path being traveled rather than a UI connector.

It can move through Arrival, respond subtly to interaction, connect
moments, and participate in the final delivery.

## 9. Moment I --- Arrival

### Purpose

Invite, rather than explain.

### Visual

A restrained editorial environment with strong negative space and subtle
atmosphere.

The mythology is abstract and understated.

The wing appears subtly.

The trajectory exists somewhere within the composition.

### Typography

**Hermes**

*THE HERALD*

> **Before the result, there is the journey.**

**Begin the Journey →**

### Feeling

**Stillness → Curiosity → Recognition → Invitation**

The intended thought:

> *"What exactly am I about to enter?"*

## 10. Moment II --- Commitment

The user selects:

> **Begin the Journey →**

There is no abrupt page load, spinner, or hard cut.

> **The entire editorial world subtly transforms around the user.**

The trajectory changes direction. Typography recedes. The composition
shifts. The atmosphere changes enough to communicate movement.

The user should feel that they have moved forward within the same world.

Then Hermes speaks:

> **"Tell me who you are."**

The journey begins.

## 11. Chapter Interaction

Use a **hybrid progressive interaction**.

Each chapter contains several related questions, but they reveal
progressively rather than appearing as one giant form.

**Hermes asks → field appears → user answers → next field reveals →
chapter completes**

Poetic language must never distort the actual data being collected.

For example:

> **"Tell me who you are."**

Then precise fields such as:

-   Age
-   Sex
-   High School Type
-   Scholarship

The poetry establishes meaning.

The interface establishes clarity.

## 12. Chapters

### Chapter I --- The Student

> **"Tell me who you are."**

### Chapter II --- Home

> **"Where do you live?"**

### Chapter III --- Family

> **"Who stands beside you?"**

### Chapter IV --- Learning

> **"How do you learn?"**

### Chapter V --- Progress

> **"Where are you now?"**

The raw dataset variables remain accurate; the chapter framing gives
them human meaning.

## 13. Hermes' Interaction Rules

Hermes should:

-   Guide
-   Observe
-   Ask
-   Reflect
-   Deliver

Hermes should not:

-   behave like a conventional chatbot
-   explain every variable
-   celebrate artificially
-   pretend certainty
-   make moral judgments
-   overwhelm the user
-   speak after every input

Acknowledgements should be rare and purposeful.

Silence is part of Hermes' personality.

## 14. Moment IV --- Reflection

After submission, Hermes should not immediately show:

> "Prediction Complete."

The world becomes quieter.

The messenger departs.

A short reflective pause creates anticipation without becoming a generic
loading screen.

## 15. Moment V --- Delivery

The messenger returns.

Hermes delivers an insight that feels considered rather than printed.

Possible language direction:

> **"I have considered what you shared."**

Then:

> **"This is what I found."**

The prediction is displayed clearly.

The experience must preserve:

> **The prediction is an estimate, not a verdict.**

Hermes can communicate what the model suggests.

He cannot tell the student what they will become.

## 16. Non-Negotiables

Hermes must never regress into:

-   Generic AI dashboards
-   Giant forms
-   Generic purple cards
-   Excessive rounded containers
-   Flashy gradients
-   Unnecessary particles
-   Literal Greek temples
-   Archaeological museum aesthetics
-   Fantasy-game mythology
-   Fake AI mysticism
-   Exaggerated chatbot behavior
-   Recruiter-focused gimmicks

If an element does not support the experience, remove it.

## 17. The Core Sentence

> **"That didn't feel like filling out a form."**

And beneath it:

> **"Someone cared deeply about building this."**

## 18. Guiding Principle

> **We don't design pages. We design moments worth remembering.**

And for Hermes:

> **Before the result, there is the journey.**
